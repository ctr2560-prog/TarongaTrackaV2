01What ZooSnooz is

Students become night researchers. At each of five animals they read a keeper insight, use an on-screen instrument to gather their own data, answer one question about what they found, write an observation, and film a short piece to camera. The clips are joined into a documentary they keep.

The app does the structure, the timing and the marking. Your job on the night is movement, pacing and encouragement — not running the technology.

Animals can be done in any order. The app tracks progress and nobody can lose their place by walking away mid-activity.

The lion and tiger instruments run on a timer, so those two need the group settled and quiet. The other three can be done at whatever pace suits.

04Running the night

Before you start

  • The teacher gives every student the class code. Students pick an animal alias — no real names are ever entered.
  • Check phones are charged. A full run uses the camera for roughly ten minutes.
  • Ask them to turn auto-lock off, or at least to the longest setting.

During

  • Split the group so not everyone is at the same animal. Any order works.
  • The lion and tiger need quiet and stillness for thirty seconds.
  • Red torchlight preserves night vision and disturbs the animals far less than white.

The documentary, scoring & what to do if something goes wrong

05The documentary

When a student has finished the animals they are doing, they tap Finish Session. The app joins their clips into one short film — a title card, a card for each animal, their own footage with the Taronga branding over it, and a closing card. It takes about 45 seconds and is built on the phone itself.

Tell students

  • Keep the screen on and the app in front while the film builds. If the phone locks it pauses and waits, so it simply takes longer.
  • Do not reload or close the tab during the build.
  • They can watch it, save it, and the teacher can share a private link afterwards.

What is kept

  • Every clip uploads as it is filmed. Nothing depends on the film succeeding.
  • Raw clips are deleted after 12 months; the finished film is kept.
  • Teachers see every clip and film in their class portal.
06How points work

Each animal is worth up to 120 points. The written observation is marked out of 15 across three areas and scaled to 100; a question answered correctly first time adds 20.

AreaWhat earns marksOut of
BehaviourDescribing what the animal is actually doing — moving, resting, hiding, calling5
DetailNaming what they can see — claws, horn, fur, shadow, sounds, surroundings5
WritingLength and clarity, with expectations set by the class stage5
QuestionCorrect on the first attempt — retries still teach, but do not score20

Marking adjusts to the class stage: a Year 3 answer is held to Year 3 expectations, not the same bar as Year 11.

07If something goes wrong

The film has the cards but none of the footage

The screen will say why. If it mentions opening in Safari, do that — some browsers will not let the app read video. Every clip is already saved either way, so nothing is lost; they can rebuild the film later.

Common fixes

  • Camera will not start: the browser is blocking it. Reload and allow camera access.
  • Microphone unavailable on the lion: tap continue and watch quietly. It does not block progress.
  • A student has lost their place: their work is saved against their alias. Rejoining with the same class code and alias restores it.

Worth knowing

  • No student ever enters a real name.
  • Filming is opt-out, arranged by the school. A student not being filmed operates the camera for their group.
  • The app works without reception for everything except uploading; clips catch up when signal returns.
